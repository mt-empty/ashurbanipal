# Conformance seed: dialect parity for MySQL/SQLite

> **Status:** OPEN · **Area:** conformance, infra

**Problem:** the conformance seed exists in three files that are not kept
in sync. `conformance/seed/seed.sql` is generated by `tools/seed-gen` and
byte-checked against `.devcontainer/db/init/01-seed.sql` by
`tools/check-seed-sync.sh`. `conformance/seed/seed.mysql.sql` and
`conformance/seed/seed.sqlite.sql` are **hand-authored**, carry a
**reduced FK set** (missing `payments.processed_by_user_id`,
`payments.related_event_id`, `audit_log.session_id`, `audit_log.event_id`
vs. the Postgres seed), and nothing verifies they match — `check-seed-sync.sh`
skips them, and `seed.mysql.sql`'s own header says so.

**Why it matters:** the cross-dialect conformance guarantee is weaker than
"the suite is green" implies. The Rust/Postgres path tests against a rich
fixture; MySQL and SQLite test against a thinner one. This first bit when
adding [[14-incoming-fk-references]] (§5.9 `/api/tables/referenced-by`):
`referenced_by` for `users` returns 8 entries on Postgres and 7 on
MySQL/SQLite, so the conformance test cannot assert the entry list or its
length cross-dialect — it falls back to presence-based checks on the three
FKs all three seeds happen to share. Any future FK-graph feature inherits
the same constraint.

**Options:**
- Teach `tools/seed-gen` to emit MySQL and SQLite dialects (one generator,
  three outputs) and byte-check all three, the way `seed.sql` is checked
  today. Most work, best result — the seeds can't drift.
- Keep the hand-authored files but add a CI check that diffs the FK graph
  (referencing table.column → referenced table.column, plus PK sets)
  across the three seeds and fails on a mismatch. Cheaper; catches
  divergence without forcing generator work.
- Bring the two hand-authored seeds up to full FK parity once, manually,
  and add the diff check to keep them there.

**Impact / constraints:**
- `conformance/seed/VERSION` and `conformance/runner/tables.rs`'s
  `SEEDED_TABLES` (`[&str; 15]`) are coupled to any seed table change; a
  pure FK-parity fix touches neither.
- The dialect seeds also differ structurally on purpose (`seed.sqlite.sql`
  has no `other_schema`/`warehouse`; `seed.mysql.sql` has no `warehouse`)
  — parity here means the FK graph *within* the schemas each seed does
  define, not identical files.
